Electromagnetic induction

Change the magnetic field through a coil and a potential difference is induced across it. Start with a magnet and a coil, then see the same idea at work in an AC generator and a dynamo.

Part 1A magnet and a coil

Exam-style explanation

Induction

  1. The wire or coil moves through the magnetic field of the permanent magnet.
  2. So the wire or coil experiences a changing magnetic field.
  3. This induces a potential difference across the wire or coil.
  4. If the wire or coil is in a closed circuit, a current flows.

What it takes

A potential difference is induced across the coil only while the magnetic field through it is changing. Leave the magnet still, inside or outside the coil, and the meter reads zero however strong the magnet is.

Making it bigger

Move the magnet faster, or put more turns on the coil. Try Fast, then 10 turns. A stronger magnet does the same. Dragging the slider yourself, the quicker you drag, the further the needle swings.

Reversing it

Pull the magnet out and the needle swings the other way. Turn the magnet round so the other pole leads and it reverses too. Do both and it is back the way it started.

It opposes the change

The induced current turns the coil into an electromagnet. Push a north pole in and the near end of the coil becomes a north pole, which repels it. Pull it out and that end becomes a south pole, which attracts it. Either way you have to do work to keep the magnet moving, and that work is where the electrical energy comes from.

Part 2A coil turning between magnets

The same idea, turning

Here the magnets stay still and the coil moves. As it turns, the field through the coil keeps changing, so a potential difference is induced the whole time it is turning. The current is largest when the long sides cut straight across the field, and zero when they move along it.

The AC generator

Each end of the coil is joined to its own slip ring, and each ring stays on its own brush. The current in the coil reverses every half turn, and the rings pass it straight on, so the meter swings one way and then the other. That is alternating current.

The dynamo

The ends of the coil are joined to the two halves of a split ring. The current in the coil still reverses every half turn, but at that moment the halves swap brushes. The current through the meter always flows the same way. It rises and falls, but it never reverses: direct current.

Turning it faster

The field through the coil changes more quickly, so the current is larger. Each turn also takes less time, so the peaks come closer together. Compare Slowly and Fast on the graph in Fig 4.

Reversing it

Turn the coil the other way, or swap the magnets, and every induced current reverses. Run it the other way, with a cell in place of the meter, and it is a motor: see the simple motor.
