Inside a hairdryer

A hairdryer does two jobs. A small DC motor drives a fan that pushes air through the barrel, and a coil of resistance wire heats that air on its way out. Turn the dryer round, hide its casing, then take the motor apart.

Fan and motor

The fan sits on the motor's shaft, just inside the air inlet. The motor is a low-voltage DC type, so the mains supply reaches it through a dropper coil and four diodes (the bridge rectifier: the row of four small blocks on the heater board).

Heater

Nichrome wire wound on a cross of mica boards. Mica does not conduct and does not burn. Low uses one element (900 W), High uses both (1800 W).

Safety parts

The square block on the board is a bimetallic thermostat. It opens the circuit if the air stops moving and the heater gets too hot. The long block beside it is a one-shot thermal fuse.

Exam-style explanation

The motor effect in a motor

  1. There is a current in the coil.
  2. This creates a magnetic field around the coil.
  3. This interacts with the permanent magnetic field.
  4. This causes a resultant force on the coil.
  5. The direction of the force is opposite on either side of the coil.
  6. So the coil rotates.

Three coils, two brushes

The coils are not wired to the terminals one each. They form a closed ring, and the brushes tap that ring at two points. From the + brush the current splits: one route goes through a single coil, the other through the remaining two in series. All three coils carry current all the time.

Using Fleming's left-hand rule

Each coil is wound round its arm, so its two long sides run along the shaft: in Fig 3 they point out of and into the page (the dot and the cross). Apply the rule to those sides, with the field running from N to S. The stripes across each coil are only its end turns. They lie in the page, and the forces on the front and back end turns cancel.

Two forces make a turning pair

The current in the two sides of a coil is in opposite directions, so the two forces are opposite. Together they twist the coil, exactly as in the one-coil motor. Each of these coils is that same rectangular coil, moved out from the shaft on its arm.

What the computed field shows

Switch Field to Computed for this. Iron carries flux far more easily than air, so the lines crowd into whichever arms face the magnets, cross the air gap almost at right angles to the iron, and return round the steel can. The copper sits beside the arms where the field is weak. In this model only about a fifth of the turning force acts on the copper. The rest acts on the iron.

Cogging

Three iron arms passing two magnets also give a pull that has nothing to do with the current: the arms prefer to sit lined up with the magnets. It adds to the drive for part of each sixth of a turn and opposes it for the rest, averaging zero. You can feel it as notches when you turn a small motor by hand.

What changes as it turns

Every sixth of a turn a gap in the commutator passes a brush. That brush moves on to the next segment, so a different coil becomes the one on its own, and one coil has its current reversed. Pause Fig 2 and drag the Armature slider to step through it.

Where the current goes

Follow the lead from the + terminal: it reaches one carbon brush, crosses to the copper commutator, runs through the armature coils, and leaves by the other brush to the − terminal.

The coils sit in the field between the N and S magnets, so the forces on them turn the armature.

Commutator and brushes

The brushes stay still. The commutator turns with the armature. Each time a gap passes a brush, the current in one coil reverses, so its pole swaps between north and south.

Why it keeps turning

A pole swaps to south just as it reaches the S magnet. Like poles repel, so it is pushed on round instead of stopping there. Pause it and drag the Armature slider slowly past a magnet to watch the tint change.

Why three poles?

The one-coil motor in a textbook has two dead spots each turn where the force is zero. With three coils at 120° there is always at least one in a good position, so the motor starts from any angle.
