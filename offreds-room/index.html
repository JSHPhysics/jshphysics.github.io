Notes for the teacher
  • The page quotes nothing from the novel. Students find and copy the words themselves, so they need the text in front of them.
  • Chapter references follow the standard numbering: the room in chapter 2, the cupboard in chapter 9, the cushion in chapter 10, the study conversation in chapter 29. Check them against your edition before the lesson.
  • The model has three views. "An ordinary bedroom" adds ten things a bedroom usually has; they are for comparison and the novel does not list them. "What was removed" shows only the light fitting, because that is the one thing chapter 2 says was taken out. The picture glass is left for students to argue about.
  • The reason these things were removed is self-harm, and the chapter 29 layer returns to it. The page never describes it, so students meet it in the novel's own words. You may want to set the chapter buttons yourself and brief the class first.
  • The cupboard is drawn but the narrator never mentions it in chapter 2. Its first question uses that gap to open a discussion of narrative reliability.
  • Writing is kept in the browser on that device only. "Copy my notes" gives students plain text to paste into their own document.
